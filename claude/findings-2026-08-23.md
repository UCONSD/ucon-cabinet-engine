## H.96 (printed p.245-249) — two derived rules, one page, and a check that did not fail

**1. The widest-unit sentence is boilerplate.** *"In compound wall units, the
dish-drainer rack is fitted in the widest unit"* is printed at H.36, H.48 and
H.60 beside one-rack positions, and is **absent** at H.84 beside a two-rack one.
That looked like a fact and was written down as one: *"with two racks there is
nothing to place and the sentence is absent — the rule and its absence agree."*
Printed p.249 holds `PF1092`/`PF1292` — H.84's twin in every respect, same
folding doors, same 60+45 and 60+60 splits, same **two** racks — **and it prints
the sentence.** It also appears at H.60 beside *symmetric* modules (`PD0914` is
45+45) where there is no widest unit at all, and is absent from `PC1811` (90+90)
where a dimensioned 90 cm rack answers instead. **Its presence is now recorded
per position in `widest_unit_rack_rule`, read off the page, derived from
nothing.**

**2. "A compound never carries the width restriction" was five pages agreeing.**
*"Cannot be reduced in width"* is on every simple dish-drainer position and on no
compound — through H.36, H.48, H.60, H.72 and H.84. The H.96 compound carries
it.

**The check went green while the note beside it was false.** The old rack check
classified `PF1092` as *one rack per module* and never looked at the sentence, so
nothing failed. **A check can only fail on what it looks at** — the rewrite looks
at the recorded sentence and never at a proxy for it, and it ends with an
anti-derivation clause: two compounds with the *same* rack count must disagree
about the sentence, or somebody re-reads the pages before deriving anything.

**Three roll-calls became structural in the same pass** (rule 18): the stacked
splits pinned `[[360,360],[480,360]]` and H.96 added `[480,480]`; the microwave
niches pinned two literal codes; the width restriction pinned the compound half.

**`PF0696` is the first niche with a side-hinged door.** `PE0696` and `PG0696`
are top-hung and unhanded; `PF0696` is headed *"Wall unit with door — 1 rh or lh
door"* and **is** handed. The name of a position says nothing about its front.

## H.120 (printed p.252-254) — a false figure in five files, and a rule that never existed

**1. THE N_ELLE HEIGHTS ARE IN THE FILE AND NOT IN THE BOOK.** Five wall files
recorded `overall_height_n_elle_mm` — 382, 502, 622, 742, 862 — and four of them
said *"the elevation carries"* it. It does not. `pdftotext -bbox` puts `N- Elle`
and the figure at a normal **7,5 pt over the perspective drawing** on every wall
page; **rendering exactly that box at 400 and at 600 dpi shows blank paper.** It
is white text, or a hidden layer for the N_Elle edition. The fifth note, at
H.48, had already noticed the 170-dpi render did not show it and blamed the
resolution — **that guess was wrong too, and it is the reason the others went
unchecked for five days.**

The numbers stay, relabelled: **a document lead below source trust, used for
nothing, and not to be quoted to Elda as a catalog dimension.** A check now
requires every one of them to say `NOT PRINTED` and to equal its family height
plus 22. **Rule 10 said the render is the page; this is the first time the text
layer claimed something the page does not.**

**2. "THE SUFFIX MEANS X" WAS NEVER TRUE.** H.120 puts `01` and `00` on the
plain units and `31` and `30` on the boiler housings — the opposite of H.72,
H.84 and H.96 — and **the depth goes with the meaning**: `PF0601` is a boiler
housing 620 deep, `PJ0601` an ordinary door 350 deep. A derived suffix would not
mislabel a box, it would build it **270 mm out**.

**The check corrected the note that commissioned it.** H.120 was first written
up as a clean swap of a rule holding everywhere else. Asked to list every
ambiguous suffix, the check answered **four**, and `00` with **three** readings:
a single top-hung door at H.36/H.48/H.60, a boiler-housing pair at
H.72/H.84/H.96, a plain pair at H.120. `30` needs the width digits too — it is
the pull-out door at W.15 and a plain pair at W.60/90. **There was no rule to
swap.**

**3. The index under-prints a second time, and the handling held.** The wall
chapter index never names a dish-drainer section at H.120. Printed p.254 is held
as a **page** of the section whose range contains it, so `dish_drainer_h120.json`
stamps its rows *Wall units H. 120* — rule 1 untouched, the page not lost. Same
shape as p.435 inside the filler chapter.

**4. The quieter ones.** `PJ0696` is the section height minus 360 for the fourth
time (1200−360=840) and the second niche with a side-hinged door. Its page
prints *"1 rh or **sx**"*, mixing English with the Italian *sinistra*, and *"1
microwave oven niche"* without the **adaptable** the other three carry. Both kept
as printed. H.120 is **the only wall family with no folding position at all**.

## Base H.78, printed p.37-38 — the catalog has been printing two symbols at us all along

**1. THERE IS A LEGEND ON PRINTED p.19 AND WE HAD NEVER READ IT.** Two
pictograms sit beside every depth band in every base code table: a
cabinet-in-a-bracket is **"Hung version"**, a flame on a burner is **"Provisions
for hob"**. They are printed *art* — `pdftotext` cannot see them — which is how
five days of extraction went straight past them.

**The hung glyph is a printed statement of a fact the engine derives.**
`Generator.wall_hung_available?` says every base or tall cabinet can be hung
unless a row objects. Printed p.37 prints two positions whose last line is *"not
available wall hung"*, and whose left margin drops the *"Surch. for wall-hung
version on page 548"* that every other base position carries. Checked across
p.36, p.39 and p.40: every position already extracted **does** carry that line,
so nothing already held is wrong — **the derivation was right by luck.**

**And the escape hatch could not be reached.** The guard reads `unit['wall_hung']
== false`; the loader never carried the key from the registry file into the unit,
so from the day it was written no data could have triggered it. Fixed in
`50_registry.rb`, and the check that proves it fails without the fix.

**The hob glyph is new data with no home.** Recorded as
`hob_provisions_depths_mm` on the positions of p.37 and p.38 — d.62 and d.67,
never d.35 — read by nothing, and recorded **only** on the pages actually looked
at. **A sweep of printed p.36, p.39, p.40 and p.42 is owed**, and their silence
means *unread*, not *absent*. `_manifest.json` carries the legend under
`page_symbols` with the sweep written into it.

**2. ONE DESCRIPTION, TWO ARTICLES — TWICE ON ONE PAGE.** Printed p.38 prints
*"Base unit with drawer and doors"* as suffix **40** and again as **46**, at the
same widths, the same depths and **the same price in every band**. *"Base unit
with drawer and door"* is **41** on p.37 and **47** on p.38. What separates them
is the elevation and nothing else: 40 and 41 print two — handle 19,5/58,5 and
gola 16,5/55,5 — while 46 and 47 print one, 19,5/55,5 with the groove *between*
the fronts and none under the worktop. **Anything keying a position by its
printed description would merge articles the factory sells apart**, and the price
column would not catch it.

**3. TWO CONTRACT GAPS, NAMED.** Thirteen of the twenty-eight codes on these two
pages are held and **not buildable**, for reasons that are ours and not the
page's:

- **A band above a split.** A drawer front across the full width with **two**
  doors beneath it. `front_layout` v2.1 offers `single`, `vertical_split`,
  `horizontal`, `corner_door` — none nests. `Export.fronts_in` would count two
  fronts where the page prints three, and a handle count that is quietly wrong is
  worse than one that is refused.
- **An article printed only in the gola execution.** 19,5 + 55,5 = 750, the
  family's gola door height, where a handle execution must sum to 780. That
  reading is **ours** (rule 4), forced by arithmetic. `door_versions` is a
  **family** key, so there is no way yet to say one code exists at 75 and not
  at 78.

Both are recorded on the articles, with their heights kept, and a check now
refuses to let `front_layout_incomplete` and `buildable` drift apart.

**4. And a typo the registry keeps.** The page heads two positions *"Base unit
w/door and pull-out **tabe**"* and prints *"pull-out table"* in the line below,
on the same position, twice. The registry keeps the catalog's words.

## Printed p.48 — the same gap again, from the other end of the book

**Three dishwasher-door executions, six codes, and all six held and not
buildable for the reason printed p.38 found ten pages earlier.**

`V88559`/`V88659` print **36 / 36**. `V88566`/`V88666` print **19,5 / 55,5**.
`V88569`/`V88669` print **16,5 / 55,5**. The family door height is 780; those
pairs sum to **720, 750 and 720**. Subtract one 30 mm recess and you get 750,
subtract two and you get 720 — so **all three are gola panels**, which the page's
own prohibition block supports: none of them is available with an L-shaped grip
recess, and the first names *"1 aluminum profile for straight groove use"*. The
handle execution of this door is `V80530` on printed p.47, one front, 78/75.

**That reading is ours** (rule 4). What it runs into is not the reading but the
same missing axis: `door_versions` is a **family** key. **Nineteen codes in two
sections now wait on one decision** — thirteen base units and six appliance
panels — and a check now requires every one of them to name that reason in the
same words, so that when the axis is finally narrowed to an article, whoever
does it finds the whole backlog in one grep.

**And a third instance of one description naming two articles.** `V88566` and
`V88569` print the same four lines, the same two widths and the same price in
every band. Only the elevation differs. That is now twice on printed p.38 and
once here — **three times in one day**, in two different chapters.

**One word apart.** Printed p.47 heads its door *"for fully-integrated
dishwasher"*; p.48 heads all three *"for integrated dish-washer"*. Two words, two
different article families, `V805xx` against `V885xx`.

## Group 0 — the plan was wrong about its own remainder

`claude/extraction-plan-2026-08-23.md` §5 called Group 0 *"the clean remainder…
no new family, no new grammar, no new concept — the warm-up that proves the
current pipeline still works end to end"*, and listed printed p.37, p.38, p.48,
p.112 and p.115.

**Three of the five were exactly that. The other two are not extractable at
all**, and the manifest — written when those pages were actually read — already
said so:

- **printed p.112** — every type on the page is defined by an interior mechanism
  the page names and does not code: Arena Style Plus pull-out doors and trays,
  an ETOUCH electrical opening with a power adapter priced in the margin with no
  article. The envelope could be drawn today; the thing the article *is* could
  not be recorded.
- **printed p.115** — the two fridge units belong to the appliance module, and
  both carry a Servo Flex mechanism and a power adapter priced without codes.

**The plan sized Group 0 by page count; the manifest knew by page content.** The
lesson is small and worth keeping: a plan written from an index is an estimate of
*volume*, never of *blockers*. Group 0 is closed as far as reading can take it,
and what remains of it is two pages waiting on `registry/cesar/options/` and the
appliance module — not on transcription.
