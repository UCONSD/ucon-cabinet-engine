between base units and tall units — no kit appears in both halves.

## What this settles

**Kit availability is height-banded, and the bands are not uniform.**
`KIT-F` exists only at base height **84** — at 78 and 66 it is not offered. Every
other row offers the same set across all its heights. This is a constraint the
Object Contract has to be able to express: a companion whose availability
depends on the parent's height, not only on its type.

**Only two widths exist, 45 and 60.** No kit is offered at any other width.
`996OL6 … LEGRABOX PROF.50 CM.60` on estimate 2026/30831 is the W.60 member,
seated in a W.600 column — consistent.

**The heights are our known rows.** 66 / 78 / 84 for bases and
138 / 198 / 210 / 222 / 234 for tall units match the catalog's own height series:
`CE0631` is H.198, `CR0635` is H.210, and 222 and 234 are the two stacks that
reach a 3048 ceiling on Avenida Primavera. Nothing new, but it means the page
can be joined to the section files on height without a translation table.

**`L` versus `O` is the top element, and only the top element.** At all four tall
heights the two drawings differ in exactly one place: `KIT-L` carries a shallow
strip at the top of the kit block, `KIT-O` carries a full-height one in its
place. Below it the two are identical. This agrees with the article description
on the estimate — `KIT TIPO 'L' 1 CASSETTO E 3 CESTONI INTERNI` — one shallow
drawer over three large ones; `O` reads as four large ones.

## Correction, dated — supersedes a guess made on 2026-08-24

On 2026-08-24 Elda wrote, of the kits she was not offered:

> "The other drawer sets might be for shorter structures."

**That guess is wrong, and p.568 replaces it.** The kits she was not offered are
not for shorter columns — they are the BASE unit kits `D`, `E` and `F`, a
different half of the page. Her 2026-08-25 letter states the rule correctly:

> "L and O are the standard options for tall units. The only difference is the
> height of the top drawer."

The 2026-08-24 sentence stays on the record because it is why this page was
opened. It is marked wrong, not removed.

**Method note.** The composition of `L` and `O` was answerable from the printed
page and was asked of the dealer instead. Read the source first; the letter was
still worth sending for the pricing question it carried, but this half of it was
avoidable.

## What this page does NOT answer — do not infer it from here

**Depth.** p.568 carries no depth at all. `PROF.50` in the ordered article did
not come from this page; it comes from the price table on p.569, which prices
every kit at both d.50 and d.60. **Why Metron seated a `PROF.50` kit in a d.620
carcass is still open**, and it is a p.569 question, not an Elda question.

**Exact drawer counts for the base kits.** `D`, `E` and `F` differ in how their
bands are divided, but the bands on the received image are small and a
miscount of one is easy. Take the composition from the article descriptions on
p.569, which name the parts in words, not from this drawing.

**Code grammar.** `996OL6` is the only kit article we hold. The `996` prefix also
carries `996811` (servo drive transformer, USA) and `996805` (distribution
extension) on the same estimate, so `996` looks like the mechanisms and
accessories block rather than anything kit-specific. Whether `OL6` decomposes as
kit-`L` at width `60` is a **hypothesis, not a reading** — it needs a second
article from p.569 before it becomes a rule.
