## 1. The finding: a door version can be an ARTICLE fact

Every base page in this book prints two elevations over its code tables — the
full door height, and the same height less 30 mm where the top of the front
goes into the gola profile. Four families have now printed that pair and we
have stored it four times as a FAMILY fact: 39 / 36, 48 / 45, **58,5 / 55,5**,
78 / 75. `core/50_registry.rb` reads `door_versions` off the family;
`core/80_panel.rb` offers the choice to every article in it.

**printed p.32 prints ONE elevation over its first position.** *Base unit with
pull-out door* — `B30100`, `B40100`, `B40300` — is drawn once, at 58,5, with
the second profile's space empty beside it. The two door positions below it on
the same page draw both. So do both positions on printed p.33 and all four on
printed p.35.

And the same position one family up, *Base unit with pull-out door* on printed
p.36, draws **78 / 75**.

That is not a page being terse. It is one position in one family declining a
choice its neighbours on the same page declare, and the twin of that position
in another family taking it.

**What it means for the 19 not-buildable H.78 codes.** They wait on exactly
this axis from the opposite direction: `base_drawer_door_gola_only` and its
three siblings print a gola elevation whose parts sum to 750 and not 780, and
the registry cannot say *this article exists at 75 and not at 78* because
`door_versions` is a family key. Here the registry cannot say *this article
exists at 58,5 and not at 55,5*, for the same reason. **One answer settles
both**, which is why this went to Elda as **Q8** rather than into a note.

Nothing was changed to accommodate it. `door_versions` is still a family key,
the panel still offers 55,5 on all three codes, the absence is recorded in
`base_pull_out_door.door_version_note`, and the check
*THE PULL-OUT DOOR IS THE ONE POSITION THIS FAMILY DRAWS ONCE* pins the shape
of the gap so that the day the axis narrows to an article, it is a failing
check and not a forgotten sentence.

## 2. The hob glyph is per (position x depth), not per page

printed p.19's legend gives two glyphs that sit beside the depth bands: a
cabinet-in-bracket for *Hung version* and a flame for *Provisions for hob*.
Read off the renders:

| page | hung | hob |
|---|---|---|
| p.32 pull-out door | d.35, d.62 | **none, including d.62** |
| p.32 base unit with door | all three | **d.62 only** |
| p.32 base unit with doors | all three | **d.62 only** |
| p.33 both positions | d.35 / d.47 / d.62 as printed | none at any depth |
| p.34 compact oven | **none** | d.62 |
| p.35 all four sink positions | both depths | none |

The mark travels with the position AND the depth, and the pull-out door at
d.62 is the proof: the same family, the same depth, the same page, one row
higher, and no flame. A rule that had said *d.62 gets the hob mark* would have
been true for four positions out of five on one page and wrong on the fifth.

`hob_provisions_depths_mm` is recorded on the two p.32 door positions and on
the compact oven, and nothing in `core/` reads it yet — the check that says so
now accepts p.32 and p.34 alongside p.37 and p.38.

## 3. Three ways a page refuses the hung version, and only one of them is words

printed p.34, *Base unit for built-in compact oven*, refuses it three times
over: **"– not available wall hung"** as the position's own last line, **no
hung glyph** on its depth band, and **no** *Surch. for wall-hung version on
page 548* line in its margin, which carries only the side panel and the feet.

Forty-six articles in the registry now refuse the hung version. **Four of them
say so in words** — these two, and the two H.78 pull-out-table positions on
printed p.37. Everything else refuses by leaving a glyph off, which is an
absence, and an absence has to be re-read every time a page is re-rendered.
That difference is now a check of its own.

## 4. The gola pair that does not sum to the gola height

*Base unit with jumbo drawers* on printed p.33 — which the description says is
one drawer above one jumbo drawer — prints **19,5 / 39** on the full elevation
and **16,5 / 36** on the gola one. 19,5 + 39 = 58,5 and the family checks out;
16,5 + 36 = **52,5**, which is neither 58,5 nor the 55,5 the family's gola
height is.

It is not an error, and the 400-dpi crop shows why: the gola elevation is drawn
with **two** recesses, one under the worktop and one **between the two fronts**.
Each opening front gives up its own 30 mm. 165 + 30 + 360 + 30 = 585.

The registry already had the vocabulary for this — `gola_stack_top_to_bottom`
with `zone` entries, written for H.78's stacked positions on printed p.39 and
p.40 — so this cost nothing to record, and the arithmetic is now a check.
Worth writing down anyway, because the printed pair is the kind of number that
gets copied into a spec sheet and then does not add up.

## 5. The appliance positions do not use the chapter's width index

printed p.34 prices the compact-oven housing as **`B48698` at W.60** and
**`B48999` at W.90**. This chapter's width index reads 06 and 09; these read 86
and 89, and the trailing pair moves too — 98 at W.60 and 99 at W.90.

Swept the whole book for the tails: **`B88698` / `B88798` / `B88999`** at H.78
d.62 and **`B98698` / `B98798` / `B98999`** at H.78 d.67, and the d.62 trio is
priced identically to the H.58.5 pair in every band it shares.

So the two-character prefix is still the (family, depth) key — that part of the
grammar holds — and the four digits behind it are an **article number shared
across families**, not a width field plus a type suffix. Recorded in
`code_grammar.base_units.appliance_article_numbers` as READ, NOT DECODED.

Note also that H.58.5 prints **W.60 and W.90 only** where the H.78 chapter's
same position prints 60 / 75 / 90.

**The article itself is held and NOT buildable.** Its front is *"1 front that
can be modified to suit the appliance"* and the only number on its elevation is
a **minimum** — 1, with a red warning that a Frame-grip front cannot go below
H 7 cm. A front whose height the appliance decides is not a front this registry
can state, and the niche under it is the appliance module that five positions
in five chapters already wait on. Findable, orderable, not drawn.

## 6. Smaller things the pages said

- **The prefix trio is out of order for the third family running.** B3 = d.35,
  B6 = d.47, B4 = d.62. Sorting the letters would put the deepest in the
  middle. H.48 taught this (BC / BQ / BD) and H.39 before it; the check that
  ties each code's prefix to its depth is now written for H.58.5 too.
- **Suffix 00 is two different positions on one page**, exactly as at H.78:
  `B30100` at W.15 is the pull-out door, `B30900` at W.90 is the two-door box.
  Only the width field separates them. Third family to print that collision,
  and it is still a collision rather than a grammar.
- **Suffix 51 agrees across families and its content does not.** At H.78 it is
  a jumbo drawer with interior drawer**s**; here it is a jumbo drawer with
  **one** interior drawer. A suffix that travels is not a suffix that means
  the same thing.
- **Sink bases take the family's two deeper depths.** d.47 and d.62 here, d.62
  and d.67 at H.78 — no d.35 in either. Two sections agreeing; derived from
  neither.
- **One fixed-front sink position, not two — and that is evidence for Q4.**
  printed p.45 gives H.78 two positions, suffixes 90 and 91, identical in
  dimensions and price and differing only in the gola front split, and the
  source never names the distinction. printed p.35 prices the same idea once,
  suffix 80. It does not answer Q4, but whatever 90 and 91 separate is not
  something every family needs separated.
- **The width sets agree with the Guidelines.** CESAR - 1 printed p.68 draws
  H.58.5 as four columns — a full front at 15 to 120, a 19,5 + 39 split at 45
  to 120, an oven at 60 / 90, a two-door box at 90 / 120 — and printed p.32-34
  price exactly those. Two chapters agreeing is as close to confirmation as
  this book gets.
- **And the same page offers a depth the price pages do not.** Its H.58.5 depth
  list reads 35 / 47 / 62 / **67**. No d.67 row exists in the family and no
  fourth prefix exists for one. `depths_mm` records what the price pages print;
  the discrepancy went to Elda as **Q9**.
- **The plinth is 100**, read for this family off printed p.68 and not
  inherited from H.78 — the third low family read that way rather than assumed.

## 7. What this cost the checks

Six new checks, and **one old one had to be scoped**.

*the two remaining p.36 types are in: 4 pull-out door codes, 2 laundry* counted
type keys across the whole catalog. A type key is unique inside a family and
nowhere else, so the moment H.58.5 opened a `base_pull_out_door` of its own the
count went to 7 for a page that still prices exactly 4. **Rule 18 again — the
check failed for a reason its title does not mention, so the title was the
bug.** It now selects on the section first.

Everything else was additive: the code count (615 → 692), the catalog row count,
the section list, and the hob-page regex, which was written as `p.3[78]` and is
now `p.3[2478]`.
